The bookshop is tucked into a narrow lane. There is no bright sign at the door, just an old wooden entrance and opening hours written by hand on the glass. Inside, the first things I notice are the smell of paper and coffee.

The shelves are low, with breathing room between the books. The owner says the shop is more like a friend's living room: visitors can sit and read, or simply have tea without buying anything. I stayed by the window for a long while, finished an essay, and leafed through a few travel books.

> Sometimes getting lost is how we find a place worth staying in.

By the time I left, the sky had begun to dim. I bought a modestly covered collection of essays. On the walk back to the station, the streetlights came on one by one, while the bookshop windows still held a warm yellow glow.
